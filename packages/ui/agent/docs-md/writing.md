# Writing

TwoUX speaks plain UK English. Buttons start with a verb, everything is in sentence case, and every message says what happened and what to do next. AI is described by what it does, never as smart or magic. How the copy should sound is set in Voice and tone.

Kind: foundation · Group: Key principles

## Principles

- Plain English first.
- Say what happened, then what to do. Error messages should state the problem in plain language and point to a fix.
- Verbs on actions. Lead buttons with a verb that says what will happen.
- Same thing, same word, everywhere.

## Guidelines

- UK English spelling and conventions: organise, colour, licence (noun); dates as 6 November 2026 with no ordinal; numerals for all numbers including 2 to 9; commas in numbers over 999.
- Sentence case for everything: headings, buttons, tabs, menu items, column headers.
- Buttons: verb first, 2 to 4 words, a noun when the target is unclear. 'Close listing', 'Extend 30 days', 'Export CSV'. Never 'OK'; name the action.
- Errors and empty states: what happened, why if useful, one action. 'No listings match these filters. Clear filters.' Do not blame the user.
- Confirmations: past tense and a count. '3 listings closed. Undo'.
- Device-independent words: select or choose, not click.
- No exclamation marks and no 'please'.
- AI: describe the job, not the technology. 'Ask', 'Draft a reply', 'Summarise applicants'. Mark generated output with AiOrigin ('Assistant · from …') beside it, never a 'Generated by AI' badge or an AI colour. Do not write AI-powered, magic, smart or intelligent.
- Common contractions (can't, isn't, you're) are fine. Write 'not' in full when the negative is the point and could be missed: 'Do not close this window while the import runs.'

## Accessibility

- Error messages include a suggested fix when one is known.
- Button text and accessible names match, so speech users can say what they see.
- Never refer to things by position or colour alone ('the red one', 'above'); avoid directional language.

## Do

- 'Listing closed. It no longer appears on your careers page.'
- 'Couldn't save' becomes 'Changes not saved. Check your connection and try again.'

## Don't

- Do not write 'Oops! Something went wrong.'
- Do not write 'Smart suggestions' or 'AI-powered search'.
- Do not use Title Case In Buttons.
